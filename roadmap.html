02 · signals

Mean reversion, carry, then the rest

Mean reversion

The coupled lane to trend. Same universe, same cadence, same panel and costs.

  • Banked from trend. Coins reverse against the market over 1 to 2 weeks, cross zero near 3 and follow it from 4 to 6.
  • The question. Is the 1 to 2 week snap-back of a coin's return beyond the market a net-of-cost edge, and in which condition.
  • Event-driven. A trigger on a move large against the coin's own volatility, checked at every hourly close, with exits on reversion, a time stop or a stop. The calendar book is its baseline. The gate is recalibrated for event books first.
  • Every trend trial counts against it. Name the primary test and its mechanism before the first run.
  • Starts on the research package and the trial ledger, not on copies of the trend modules.

Funding carry, again

The first pass taught the method and is invalidated by it. Cost-blind, a feature zoo before a hypothesis, no calibrated gate, no trial ledger. Redone from scratch after mean reversion, on the package, the ledger and measured costs.

  • Banked from trend. Funding predicted next week's return on its own, t 3.00.
  • Banked from the first pass, as hypotheses only. Funding persists, basis drives it through the clamp, pooling across symbols beat fitting each.

Path shape

After mean reversion. Intraday, on spot first. Does the shape of the path so far say a cost-beating move follows, and in which regime.

  • The question is the probability of a winner given the shape at entry, never the shape given a winner. Every move looks like a trend in hindsight, and a shape common to winners is worthless if it is as common to losers.
  • Labels. Every candidate moment, not only the winners. Triple barrier, with take-profit and stop sized from the cost table and a time limit. Meta-labelling on top of a simple trigger.
  • Features from data up to the entry only. Truncated path signatures over several windows, with lead-lag and time augmentation. Regime state and volatility beside them.
  • Later geometry. Delay embedding and persistent homology, shapelets and the matrix profile. Signatures come first, cheaper and better grounded.
  • Extends to mean reversion. The same labels with the barriers flipped ask which shapes snap back.
  • 1m bars cover a first look. Order flow for earlier detection needs trades and depth.
  • Costs decide it. Spot round trips run about 15 to 25 bp, so the barriers sit well clear of that or the lane is fitting noise.
  • A shape search is a huge search. Every shape tried goes in the trial ledger. Purged walk-forward, the gate, then a registered holdout.
  • Spot is long only, so it harvests up-moves. Perps add the short side of every shape.
  • The honest prior is modest. Trend paid near four weeks, and intraday tends to revert.

Later lanes

  • Cross-sectional stat arb. Residual reversal, momentum and funding in one sort framework.
  • Open interest and positioning, as signals and as regime features.
  • Intraday flow and reversal, on trades and depth.
03 · costs

Cost modelling

An independent lane, so it can run beside mean reversion. Done before carry, whose edge is small enough for cost to decide it. Its output is one model charged the same in the package and in a C++ CostModel.

Today. A half-spread plus linear impact ladder, fitted on 2023 to 2026 depth snapshots, five 1% bands a side, and applied back to 2020. Each rebalance is one order against one snapshot.

Impact shape

Linear against the square-root law. Fit realised impact from aggTrades runs against size, volatility and depth, rather than reading it off coarse snapshot bands.

Costs that move

Spread and depth thin as volatility rises, which is when trend and reversal trade. Cost as a function of volatility and condition, not a weekly constant. GARCH and stochastic volatility come in here.

Execution over time

Almgren-Chriss. Price as Brownian motion, temporary and permanent impact, the cost of trading fast against the risk of waiting. Says whether to split a rebalance and over how long. The seven-slice book is a crude version of it.

Funding as a process

Mean-reverting, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck with jumps, fitted per symbol or pooled. A cost for every perp book and the return of carry, so it opens the carry redo.

  • Maker fills cannot be modelled offline. Queue position and fill intensity, Avellaneda-Stoikov and its kin, need top-of-book data the archive does not carry.
  • A cost model is judged like a signal. Fitted in fold, checked against realised fills where they exist, and its error carried into the backtest as a range, not a point.
04 · regime

Regime detection

After carry. Not fleshed out. Worth real thought before any code.

  • What a regime is here. A hidden state that changes the sign or size of a signal's edge, known from past data only, judged by what it adds to that signal's net sleeve.
  • Markov models. Hidden Markov and Markov-switching models put a probability on the state and on switching. Few states, few parameters.
  • Stochastic process views. Brownian motion with drift is the null, where trend is the drift and nothing else. Mean reversion is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck pull. Jump diffusion for crashes, stochastic volatility for clustered risk.
  • Changepoint detection, for breaks rather than recurring states.
  • The same discipline as signals. A state chosen on the full sample is hindsight. Detection is fitted in fold.

Handed over from trend.

  • Conditioning on the market pays. A search choosing from the past alone made 1.38 against 0.64 always on.
  • Trend loses after deleveraging, with open interest below its recent average.
  • The moderate month is a hypothesis only.
05 · risk

Risk research

Separate from regime. Regime says which state the market is in. Risk says how much to hold and how bad it can get. Beside or after regime.

  • Volatility and correlation forecasts, for sizing and for the risk gate's limits.
  • Tails and crash profiles. Trend gains in crashes that keep going and loses in ones that snap back.
  • The kill switch's drawdown limits, set from a model rather than a guess.

Volatility models feed cost, regime and risk alike. Built once.

06 · strategy

Strategy research

A strategy composes a signal with its regime activation, its cost and its risk, so this waits on all three.

  • Trend starts from the seven-slice book near four weeks. Capacity $100k to $1M.
  • The strict three-part rule is a second, concentrated book. Unproven, failed outside crypto, needs position caps.
  • The crash profile goes to the risk gate.

A trial ledger

Every book run records its return series to the lane's ledger, tagged primary, exploratory or registered. Effective trials and the luck bar are read from it. The trend late check ran about 5,700 books that never reached the count.

Python or C++

Signal research stays vectorised python. pandas and numpy already run in C, and a weekly panel of 400 coins is milliseconds. A port would rewrite them. Heavy sweeps reach for numba or polars first. C++ is for the data path, for anything that must match live, strategy, risk and sim execution, and for path-dependent event loops that do not vectorise.

08 · data

Data access

Free data only. The public Binance archive is the source. No paid storage.

A local store

Research caches parquet under ~/.cache, per lane. A shared local store in the platform's format, binary, zstd and partitioned, becomes the backend of the package's data module. Pull once, read everywhere.

Resume a planned run

An open milestone of the historical source. A run picks up without refetching what it already read.
